Hiring Committee
Carpenter Technology Corporation
Reading / Latrobe, Pennsylvania

Carpenter is not hiring someone merely to add analytics to maintenance. The position is designed to connect asset strategy, work execution, failure learning, MRO materials, enterprise architecture, governed data products, and AI-enabled decisions across manufacturing sites. That is a difficult mandate because each layer depends on the quality and adoption of the others.

My career has repeatedly placed me in those seams. At Compunetics, I led engineering, manufacturing, quality, customer delivery, root-cause practices, process controls, and high-consequence technical programs. At Amazon, I built operating mechanisms, standard work, throughput visibility, and escalation routines in a 24/7 customer-backward environment. Today at Vape-Jet, I connect production, purchasing, quality, ERP/Odoo workflows, engineering issues, support, telemetry, and a software-enabled machine fleet into earlier risk detection and cleaner execution. Through DudeWorth, I have also developed digital-product and AI-readiness frameworks that test value, feasibility, data readiness, governance, adoption burden, and reuse before technology earns scale.

I would not represent myself as a career SAP PM configurator or claim formal plant RCM tenure I have not held. The value I offer is the broader operating architecture: learning the reliability system quickly, partnering deeply with plant and SAP experts, translating practice into product, making data governance useful to the work, and installing decision mechanisms that connect short-term uptime value to long-term scalability.

I built an independent candidate vision at https://russelldudek.github.io/carpentertechnology/. Its Reliability Lattice, Reliability Product Review, and 120-day entry plan show how I would turn asset events into reusable evidence, govern AI by actionability and human authority, and prove one closed loop before scaling the pattern. The work is intentionally presented as a discovery hypothesis, not as a claim about Carpenter's undisclosed internal processes.

I would welcome a discussion about where Carpenter's current reliability system loses the most learning between an event, the work record, the failure explanation, the materials decision, and the next maintenance strategy.
