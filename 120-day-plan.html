120-day entry plan

Learn the system. Prove one loop. Scale the pattern.

The first four months should establish credibility without pretending the platform choice is already known.

Entry principle: Begin with one consequential, measurable reliability decision. Do not begin with an enterprise technology rollout.

Four phases

01 · Read the system

02 · Frame the product

03 · Prove the loop

04 · Scale the pattern

Days 1-30 · Read the system

Listen and observe

  • Executive objectives and capacity economics
  • Reliability engineering practices and site variation
  • Planner, scheduler, technician, operator, supply-chain, and data-team workflows
  • SAP PM / CMMS object model, integrations, and workarounds
  • Asset hierarchy, criticality, failure taxonomy, work closure, and MRO linkage

Map decisions

  • What gets worked now?
  • What gets deferred?
  • What becomes a PM change?
  • What changes a stocking strategy?
  • What escalates to engineering or capital?

Deliverables

  • Reliability product charter
  • Stakeholder and decision-rights map
  • System/data landscape
  • Top reliability decision frictions
  • Baseline measurement plan
  • Use-case selection criteria

Exit criteria

  • Problem boundary is agreed
  • One executive sponsor and one frontline product owner are clear
  • Current state is documented without assigning blame
  • No platform decision is being used to substitute for problem definition

Days 61-90 · Prove one closed loop

Select a use case with meaningful consequence, observable recurrence or burden, enough data to learn, a clear action path, and a frontline owner. Candidate examples for discovery include recurring failure elimination, condition-to-work routing, PM optimization, or critical-spare linkage.

TestEvidence
Work changedPlanning, scheduling, execution, closure, escalation, or stocking decision changed.
Data improvedThe workflow produced more complete, timely, owned, reusable evidence.
User adoptedUsers understood the recommendation, used or overrode it, and could explain why.
Outcome observedAvailability, recurrence, work quality, inventory risk, or decision latency could be compared with the baseline.
Pattern reusableThe capability can transfer across assets/sites without copying local exceptions blindly.

Days 91-120 · Scale the pattern

Institutionalize

  • Product operating cadence and backlog governance
  • Data-product owners and quality accountabilities
  • Architecture and integration guardrails
  • AI authority, evaluation, override, and learning controls
  • Site adoption playbook and local-variation rules

Portfolio decisions

  • Scale: repeatable value and adoption
  • Adapt: value with legitimate site variation
  • Hold: action or evidence not ready
  • Buy/configure: commodity capability
  • Build: differentiated workflow or integration
  • Stop: burden exceeds likely value
120-day success is not an autonomous reliability system. It is an agreed product architecture, one credible closed-loop proof, visible measurement discipline, and a reusable way to decide what scales next.
