Candidate vision forSr. Manager – Digital Product & ReliabilityCarpenter Technology · Russell Dudek

Engineer reliability at the boundaries.

High-performance systems do not fail because one component exists in isolation. They fail where context, authority, execution, materials, and learning stop connecting. The digital product leader’s job is to make those boundaries visible—and make them stronger under use.

Candidate thesis: Treat every maintenance event as a governed product transaction that must leave the next decision better informed.

The real mandate

Own the interfaces between reliability practice, digital product, architecture, data, materials, and adoption.

01

Standardize without flattening

Build reusable enterprise objects and process patterns while preserving legitimate site and asset-class variation.

02

Deliver now without creating debt

Produce near-term uptime value without turning every plant need into another disconnected point solution.

03

Govern data through the work

Improve trust by making the correct evidence easier to capture inside planning, execution, closure, and learning.

04

Use AI only where authority is clear

Prediction matters when the signal can change a bounded decision, an owner is accountable, and override learning is retained.

Candidate operating model

An event is not complete when the work order closes.

It is complete when the event has enough context to change planning, maintenance strategy, stocking, engineering, or escalation—and when the effect can be checked later.

  1. 01Frame the asset

    Identity, hierarchy, criticality, operating state, and consequence.

  2. 02Bound the signal

    Condition, threshold, timing, confidence, and false-positive cost.

  3. 03Assign authority

    Who decides, who executes, what may be automated, and what must escalate.

  4. 04Execute the work

    Plan, schedule, perform, consume materials, close, and capture useful evidence.

  5. 05Change the system

    PM task, work method, MRO strategy, asset design, standard, or capital decision.

  6. 06Verify the effect

    Recurrence, availability, work quality, adoption, economics, and unintended consequences.

Decision architecture

Govern the backlog by the decisions it improves.

No invented maturity score. No generic AI roadmap. Establish Carpenter’s baselines, then judge each product increment by the work and evidence it changes.

The strongest reasonable concern

The gap is specific. The transfer is broad.

I would not claim direct SAP PM configuration ownership or formal plant RCM tenure I have not held. I would bring verified depth in high-reliability manufacturing, root cause and process control, scaled operating systems, ERP workflow transformation, machine-fleet feedback, digital-product design, and AI governance.

The first move is not to disguise the gap. It is to pair tightly with Carpenter’s reliability and SAP PM experts while adding value at the unusually broad integration boundary this role owns.
