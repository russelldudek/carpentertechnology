The real mandate

Turn reliability from a collection of tools into an enterprise learning system.

Carpenter is asking one leader to align maintenance practice, product management, architecture, data governance, MRO economics, and AI adoption. The difficult work lives in the seams.

Standardize

Build reusable process and data patterns without erasing real differences among plants and asset classes.

Deliver now

Create near-term uptime value while avoiding local point solutions that become tomorrow's integration debt.

Trust the data

Improve data quality through better work execution, not through governance theater detached from the floor.

Use AI carefully

Apply prediction where context, actionability, ownership, and human authority are strong enough to matter.

Protect capacity

Balance spare-parts resilience, maintenance effort, inventory cost, and the economics of unavailable assets.

Reliability scales when the event becomes evidence and evidence changes the work.
Candidate thesis

The digital product should not sit beside maintenance. It should make the maintenance system easier to execute, easier to learn from, and easier to govern.

The Reliability Lattice treats every asset event as a potential connection among identity, work, condition, failure, materials, and strategy. The purpose of architecture and governance is not pristine data for its own sake. It is dependable decision quality at the moment planning, scheduling, repair, stocking, or escalation changes.

AI is the final amplifier, not the first layer. It should recommend, prioritize, explain, and detect patterns only where the operating context and human decision rights are explicit.

Identify

Asset hierarchy, criticality, ownership, and consequence.

Execute

Plan, schedule, perform, close, and capture useful evidence.

Observe

Condition, operator, process, and machine signals in context.

Explain

Failure mode, mechanism, root cause, and uncertainty.

Change

PM, work method, materials strategy, design, or escalation.

Verify

Recurrence, uptime, quality, adoption, and economic evidence.

Decision architecture

The product backlog should be governed by the decisions it improves.

This scorecard avoids invented baselines. It defines the measurement categories to establish with Carpenter's teams.

Asset outcomeAvailability, unplanned downtime, recurrence, maintenance-induced defects.Which decision changed: intervene, defer, redesign, alter PM, or retire risk?
Work qualityPlanning completeness, schedule attainment, closure quality, failure-code usefulness.Did the product reduce rework or make the next job easier to plan?
Data product qualityCompleteness, lineage, ownership, timeliness, discoverability, reuse.Can another site or use case consume the same governed object?
MRO economicsService level, stockout exposure, excess/obsolete inventory, criticality alignment.Is the inventory decision tied to asset consequence and lead-time risk?
AI adoptionRecommendations used, overridden, explained, escalated, and learned from.Is human authority explicit, and does the model improve a real workflow?

The hard objection

“You have not owned SAP PM or carried a formal reliability title.”

That concern is reasonable.

I would not claim direct SAP PM configuration ownership, deep plant-specific RCM tenure, or a title I have not held. My case is adjacent but substantial: high-reliability manufacturing and quality systems, root-cause and process control, scaled operations, ERP workflow transformation, machine-fleet feedback, digital product thinking, and AI governance.

The first operating move is not to pretend the gap away. It is to pair tightly with Carpenter's reliability and SAP PM experts, learn the existing asset and work model quickly, and add value where the role is unusually broad: connecting practice, architecture, data, product economics, and adoption into one coherent system.

Days 1-30

Read the asset, work, data, architecture, MRO, and decision landscape. Establish sponsors, product boundaries, and measures without declaring a platform answer.

Days 31-60

Define the reliability data contract and product backlog. Select one use case where recurrence, actionability, data readiness, and adoption burden can be observed.

Days 61-90

Run one closed-loop pilot from event through decision and verification. Instrument work quality, user behavior, data quality, and economic evidence.

Days 91-120

Codify the reusable pattern, governance, architecture guardrails, site adoption plan, and portfolio decisions: scale, adapt, hold, buy, build, or stop.

Open the detailed entry plan
Executive discovery

Questions I would use to find the real constraints.

Where does unplanned downtime currently become an enterprise problem rather than a plant-level problem?
Which reliability practices are truly standardized today, and which variations are legitimate?
What decisions are hardest because asset, work, condition, failure, and material data do not connect?
Where has SAP PM enabled discipline, and where have users built workarounds around it?
Which predictive-maintenance use cases have enough actionability and ownership to justify scaling?
How are spare-parts service levels balanced against inventory and obsolescence risk?
Who has decision rights over enterprise product standards versus local plant adaptation?
What would make frontline users say this digital product made the work better, not merely more visible?
